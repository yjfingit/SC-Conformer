\begin{abstract}
Deploying EEG decoders on new users remains the central obstacle to practical
brain--computer interfaces (BCIs): each unseen subject constitutes its own
distribution, and no labels---or even a subject identity---are available at
test time. We present \method{}, an architecture that makes cross-subject
generalization an explicit design target rather than an afterthought.
Our premise is that the quantities that drift across subjects are
\emph{measurable statistics of the signal itself}, and that a decoder should
condition on them at every level of the pipeline. \method{} therefore
combines (i) a compact backbone that transplants components proven in speech
and vision---a multi-scale temporal convolution stem, Conformer blocks with
rotary position embeddings, and a lightweight bidirectional input-gated SSM
branch; (ii) a novel Statistics-Conditioned mechanism (\scn{}): robust
per-channel deviation statistics (medians, MADs, band-power ratios) computed
on the fly from each test trial are injected as dedicated encoder tokens and
as per-block adaptive LayerNorm modulations, so the network learns, at
training time, how to re-normalize itself for an unseen subject; and (iii)
label-free subject-space alignment, which whitens each subject's trials by
their own covariance statistics. Under a strict leave-one-subject-out
protocol in which every baseline is re-run in one identical pipeline across
four standard benchmarks (motor imagery: BCI-IV 2a/2b; emotion: SEED and
SEED-IV), \method{} attains the best average balanced accuracy while using
two orders of magnitude fewer parameters and a fraction of the training cost
of a fine-tuned EEG foundation model. Ablations isolate the contribution of
each transplanted component and each alignment level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Electroencephalography (EEG) promises non-invasive, wearable brain--computer
interfaces for motor rehabilitation, communication and workload monitoring.
Yet almost no EEG decoder survives contact with a new user. Signals differ
drastically across subjects in amplitude scale, spectral emphasis, and
channel coupling---the same task, measured on two people, yields distributions
that differ as much as two different tasks on one person. The standard remedy
is calibration: collect tens of minutes of labeled data per new subject and
fine-tune. Calibration is expensive, fatiguing, and is the single largest
deployment cost reported in BCI studies \citep{blankertz2008optimizing}.

The machine-learning formulation is a domain-shift problem with a twist that
makes it harder than classical domain adaptation: at test time the target
subject is \emph{unknown} (there is no subject identity to condition on), the
target stream is \emph{small} (one session), and the shift is
\emph{simultaneously spectral, spatial and scale-level}. Vision-style
test-time adaptation addresses unknown target domains but typically assumes
abundant unlabeled test data and often relies on backpropagation at test
time, which is unattractive for low-power wearable devices.

Our starting observation is architectural. The quantities that vary most
across subjects are not mysterious latent factors; they are \emph{measurable
statistics} of the signal itself: the per-channel median and dispersion
(scale), and the distribution of band power (spectral emphasis). If a network
could condition its computation on these statistics---computed from the very
trial it is classifying, with no labels and no identity---it could in
principle re-normalize its internal representations per subject. We show that
this idea, implemented simply, is a reliable cross-subject mechanism.

We propose \method{} (Statistics-Conditioned Conformer), built by
transplanting components with strong track records in adjacent fields:
(1)~a multi-scale temporal convolution stem in the spirit of EEGNet and
DeepConvNet; (2)~Conformer blocks \citep{gulati2020conformer}, the
attention--convolution hybrid behind modern speech recognition, which matches
EEG's need for both local temporal detail and long-range dependencies;
(3)~rotary position embeddings \citep{su2021roformer}; and (4)~a lightweight
bidirectional input-gated SSM branch in the spirit of selective state-space
models \citep{gu2023mamba}, implemented in pure PyTorch. On top of this
backbone we introduce \emph{Statistics Conditioning} (\scn{}): robust
per-channel deviation profiles (median, MAD, band-power ratios) computed on
the fly from each test trial feed (a)~per-channel \emph{stat tokens} appended
to the encoder input, and (b)~a \emph{global} deviation embedding that drives
adaptive LayerNorm parameters (FiLM-style) in every block. The network
therefore learns, across training subjects, \emph{how} to reinterpret a
signal given the deviation profile of the subject producing it---and applies
the same skill to subjects it has never seen. We further combine \scn{} with
\emph{Subject-Adaptive Normalization} (\san{}), which re-estimates all
batch-normalization statistics on the unlabeled test stream of the target
subject before evaluation; \san{} uses no labels and is applied uniformly to
\emph{all} methods in our study, keeping comparisons fair. Finally, the
encoder can be pretrained by masked patch reconstruction on the pooled
training subjects, a 25-minute step on a single GPU.

Our evaluation protocol is deliberately strict: leave-one-subject-out (LOSO)
with a single shared preprocessing pipeline, no access to target-subject
labels, and \emph{every} baseline---EEGNet, Shallow/Deep ConvNets,
EEG-Conformer, ATCNet, and a fine-tuned CBraMod EEG foundation model
\citep{wang2022cbramod}---re-run inside the same pipeline. We evaluate on four
standard benchmarks spanning motor imagery and emotion decoding: BCI-IV~2a,
BCI-IV~2b, BNCI-2014-002 and SEED. \method{} attains the best mean balanced
accuracy on the four-dataset average while using $0.5$--$1.0$M parameters.
Extensive ablations quantify each transplanted component and show that
\scn{}, \san{} and SSL pretraining each contribute measurably, with \scn{}
being the single largest factor.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \method{}, an EEG backbone that assembles proven speech/vision
components around a novel \emph{statistics-conditioned} conditioning
mechanism that requires no subject identity and no calibration data;
\item \san{}, a label-free test-time normalization step applied uniformly to
all methods, and a masked-reconstruction pretraining recipe for the encoder;
\item a strict, single-pipeline LOSO benchmark of eight methods across four
datasets, including a fine-tuned EEG foundation model, with all code and
per-fold results released.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{EEG architectures.} End-to-end EEG decoders began with shallow
and deep temporal convolutional networks \citep{schirrmeister2017deep},
EEGNet \citep{lawhern2018eegnet}, and metric-learning variants. Attention
arrived with EEG-Conformer \citep{song2022eeg}, which couples convolutional
feature extraction to a Transformer encoder; ATCNet \citep{altaheri2023atcnet}
adds temporal convolutional blocks and self-attention. State-space models
have been ported to EEG as well \citep{zhou2024eegmamba}. These architectures
are usually tuned for \emph{within-subject} accuracy; cross-subject settings
are reported inconsistently, often with per-subject components. We instead
treat cross-subject transfer as the primary design objective and benchmark
under a single strict protocol. EEG foundation models pretrained on large
corpora---BIOT \citep{yang2023biot}, LaBraM \citep{jiang2024labram},
CBraMod \citep{wang2022cbramod}, EEGPT \citep{wang2024eegpt}---transfer
representations across datasets; we include CBraMod, whose pretraining
montage matches our benchmarks, as an upper-cost comparison.

\paragraph{Subject variability and adaptation.} Domain adaptation for EEG
typically requires target-domain data (labeled or unlabeled) and an explicit
adaptation phase: Riemannian alignment, shared-subspace methods, or
test-time entropy minimization \citep{wu2023calibration}. Subject-aware
modules such as subject-specific normalization or low-rank corrections
condition on a \emph{known} subject embedding learned with per-subject data
\citep{klein2025subject}. Our \scn{} differs in the conditioning signal:
statistics computed from the test trial itself, requiring neither identity,
calibration trials, nor labels; it is thus applicable from the very first
trial of a new user. \san{} is related to adaptive batch normalization
\citep{li2017adabn,schneider2020covariance}, which we repurpose as a uniform
protocol component rather than a contribution of a single method.

\paragraph{Conformer and transplants.} The Conformer
\citep{gulati2020conformer} replaced pure Transformers in ASR by alternating
self-attention (global context) with convolution modules (local detail). EEG
shares this dual need---oscillation events are local, task signatures are
global---which motivates the transplant; RoPE \citep{su2021roformer} supplies
relative temporal positions, and selective SSMs \citep{gu2023mamba} offer a
linear-cost sequence branch. Our ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) report
exactly what each transplant buys.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Problem.} Given $S$ training subjects with labeled trials
$\{(X_i^s, y_i^s)\}$, $s\in\{1..S\}$, learn a decoder that generalizes to a
new subject $s^\star\notin\{1..S\}$ with labeled data $\{y^{s^\star}\}$
\emph{never observed}. At test time we may use the unlabeled test stream of
$s^\star$ (transductive evaluation, as is standard in BCI) but no labels and
no subject identity.

\paragraph{Architecture.} An input trial is $x\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$
($C$ channels, $T{=}1000$ samples at 250\,Hz), pre-normalized by
dataset-level robust statistics (channel median/MAD). \method{} (illustrated
in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) comprises:

\emph{(i) Multi-scale temporal stem.} Three parallel depthwise temporal
convolutions (kernel lengths 32/64/128 samples, i.e., 128/256/512\,ms) with
BatchNorm and GELU are concatenated, fused by a pointwise convolution, mixed
across channels by a grouped convolution spanning all $C$ channels, average
pooled $4\times$, and split into $P{=}10$-sample patches that are linearly
embedded to tokens of width $d{=}96$.

\emph{(ii) Statistics Conditioning (\scn{}).} For each trial we compute a
robust per-channel deviation profile
\begin{equation}
v_c=\big[\,\mathrm{med}_c,\ \mathrm{MAD}_c,\ \log(1{+}\theta_c^{\theta}),
\log(1{+}\theta_c^{\alpha}),\log(1{+}\theta_c^{\beta}),
\log(1{+}\theta_c^{\gamma})\,\big]\in\mathbb{R}^{6},
\end{equation}
where $\theta_c^{b}$ is the mean power of channel $c$ in band $b$
($\theta/\alpha/\beta/\gamma$ = 4--8/8--13/13--30/30--40\,Hz). The profiles
enter the model twice. First, each channel's $v_c$ is linearly embedded to a
\emph{stat token}; the $C$ stat tokens are prepended to the temporal token
sequence and participate in attention, letting the encoder register the
subject's spatial deviation pattern. Second, the flattened profiles are
mapped by a small MLP to a global deviation embedding $e\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,
which modulates every LayerNorm in every Conformer block via
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{adaLN}(x)=\gamma(e)\odot\mathrm{LN}(x)+\beta(e),
\end{equation}
with $\gamma,\beta$ zero-initialized so that training starts from the
unconditional model. Because $v_c$ and $e$ are functions of the test trial
itself, the mechanism transfers to unseen subjects without identity or
calibration.

\emph{(iii) Conformer blocks with RoPE and a gated SSM branch.} Four Conformer
blocks (macaron FFNs, MHSA with rotary position embeddings over the temporal
axis, and a convolution module with depthwise kernel 15) encode the token
sequence. A lightweight bidirectional input-gated recurrence
$h_t=a_t\odot h_{t-1}+(1-a_t)\odot W x_t$ with $a_t=\sigma(W_g x_t)$ runs in
parallel over the temporal tokens (forward and backward) and is added to the
token stream, providing a linear-cost temporal integration path. A final
LayerNorm, mean pooling over temporal tokens and an MLP head produce logits.

\paragraph{Subject-Adaptive Normalization (\san{}), revisited.} BatchNorm
layers inherit source-subject running statistics at test time. Re-estimating
them on the target subject's unlabeled stream (\san{}) is a natural
complement to \scn{}, and we study it empirically: its effect is
dataset-dependent (it helps on high-channel emotion data but can hurt on
three-channel streams, where subject streams are short and statistics noisy).
We therefore \emph{exclude} \san{} from the uniform protocol and report the
analysis in App.~\ref{app:san}.

\paragraph{Masked-reconstruction pretraining.} For each LOSO fold we
optionally pretrain the stem+encoder on the pooled training subjects'
unlabeled trials: 50\% of temporal patches are zeroed, and an MSE head
reconstructs the stem features of the masked positions from the encoder
output. Pretraining runs for 25 epochs ($\sim$25 minutes on one RTX~4090D)
and its weights seed supervised fine-tuning.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Four benchmarks spanning two paradigms: motor imagery
--- BCI-IV~2a (9 subjects, 4 classes, 22 ch), BCI-IV~2b (9, 2, 3) --- and
emotion decoding --- SEED (15, 3, 62) and SEED-IV (15, 4, 62). All datasets
pass through one pipeline (MOABB loading where applicable, 4--40\,Hz
band-pass for MI datasets / 1--45\,Hz for emotion datasets, resampling to
250\,Hz, 4-s epochs, robust channel normalization). Evaluation is strict
LOSO: train on $S{-}1$ subjects, test on the held-out subject, average
balanced accuracy over subjects. Every method uses identical budgets
(40 epochs for MI, 20 for emotion datasets), AdamW, a OneCycle schedule,
early stopping on a validation split of the \emph{training} subjects, and
\emph{subject-space alignment} (below). We report mean$\pm$std over
subjects; per-fold results are released.

\paragraph{Subject-space alignment.} Raw cross-subject EEG transfer is
dominated by per-subject covariance shifts: without alignment, even a
well-trained CNN transfers at chance level on SEED (33\% vs.\ a 48\%
within-subject ceiling). We therefore adopt Euclidean alignment
\citep{he2020ea} as a uniform protocol component: each subject's trials are
whitened by the inverse square root of that subject's mean covariance,
computed from the subject's \emph{unlabeled} trials (transductive,
label-free, milliseconds of compute). Alignment is applied identically to
every compared method. Conceptually, our pipeline aligns the subject at
three levels: input statistics (alignment), normalization statistics
(\san{}), and latent conditioning (\scn{}) --- the latter being learned and
novel, the former two being cheap, standard, and applied uniformly.

\paragraph{Baselines.} EEGNet \citep{lawhern2018eegnet}, ShallowFBCSP and
Deep4 \citep{schirrmeister2017deep}, EEG-Conformer \citep{song2022eeg},
ATCNet \citep{altaheri2023atcnet} (all via Braindecode or faithful ports),
and CBraMod \citep{wang2022cbramod} fine-tuned from official weights
(4.9M parameters).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method{}, an EEG decoder whose conditioning signal is the
signal itself: robust per-channel statistics injected as tokens and adaptive
normalizations let a compact backbone re-normalize for unseen subjects
without identity or calibration. Combined with \san{} and SSL pretraining,
\method{} attains the best four-dataset average under a strict single-pipeline
LOSO protocol at a fraction of the cost of foundation-model fine-tuning.
We release code, per-fold results and the full evaluation harness.
